\begin{abstract}
Prediction \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{models in healthcareare often trained on }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{tasks in healthcare, such as diagnosing a disease or anticipating an adverse outcome, often have to be learned from }\DIFaddend small datasets, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{in which the number of records is too low for the complexity of the task, for example because a }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{because the }\DIFaddend disease is rare or because labelling records is expensive. Synthetic data generation can be a solution for such contexts, but established generators, such as oversampling techniques and generative adversarial networks, learn only from the available records\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and can therefore only reproduce }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, recombining }\DIFaddend patterns that already exist in the data. Large language models (LLMs) can \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{go beyond these patterns, because they bring }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{address this issue by adding }\DIFaddend domain knowledge acquired during pre-training\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and do not need to be }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, without being }\DIFaddend trained on the target data. When prompted directly, however, LLMs often return fewer records than requested, malformed records or values of the wrong type, so their output cannot be used as a dataset\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{without further processing}\DIFdelend . To address this issue, we present \system, a \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{system that places }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{synthetic data generation system for small healthcare datasets that combines an LLM with }\DIFaddend a control layer\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{between the LLM and the synthetic dataset. The control layer infers the names and types of the columns }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. Inferring the columns and their types }\DIFaddend from the real data, \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{the control layer }\DIFaddend checks every response of the LLM\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{against them, asks the model to correct the values that violate them, and keeps requesting }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, asks for the correction of invalid values and requests new }\DIFaddend records until the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{number set by the user }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{configured number }\DIFaddend is reached, while recording the cost of every request. \DIFdelbegin %DIFDELCMD < \system %%%
\DIFdel{was evaluated }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{In an evaluation }\DIFaddend on six public healthcare datasets\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{with four LLMs, three established generators and ten classifiers. It completed every synthetic dataset to the requested number of records}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, }\system \DIFadd{always delivered a usable synthetic dataset of the configured size}\DIFaddend , which direct prompting of the same LLM never \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{achieved, at a cost only 1.9\% higher. With the strongest LLM, adding its records }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{did, with a negligible increase in cost. Added }\DIFaddend to the training data \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{increased the average F1-score of the classifiersby 1.1 percentage points, the largest }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{of ten classifiers, its records yielded the largest average F1-score }\DIFaddend gain among the generators compared \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and }%DIFDELCMD < \system %%%
\DIFdel{was the only generator whose records never lowered the F1-score of any dataset by more than one point}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{and avoided the losses of up to 10.6 points that SMOTE, CTGAN and TVAE caused on individual datasets}\DIFaddend .
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================
Many prediction tasks in healthcare\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, such as diagnosing a disease or anticipating an adverse outcome, }\DIFaddend suffer from a lack of data. A rare disease may have only a few dozen diagnosed cases, a study conducted in a single healthcare facility is limited to the patients it can recruit, and labelling records often requires expert review. Data sharing between \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{institutions }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{facilities }\DIFaddend could increase the number of records, but it is frequently restricted by privacy regulations~\cite{rieke2020digitalhealth}\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. In this paper }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, so each facility often has to learn from its own data. In our work }\DIFaddend we refer to such datasets as \emph{small data}: datasets whose number of records is \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{low relative to what the learning task requires, which }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{too low to train a reliable prediction model, a limit that }\DIFaddend depends on the number of features, the number of classes and the frequency of the outcome~\cite{riley2019binary}. A common way to mitigate small data is synthetic data generation (SDG), which adds artificial records that follow the distribution of the real ones to the training set~\cite{qian2023synthcity}. \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{This paper focuses on SDG for prediction tasks on small healthcare datasets.
}\DIFaddend 

SDG is, however, hardest when data are small. Oversampling techniques, such as the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE)~\cite{chawla2002smote}, and deep generative models, such as the conditional tabular generative adversarial network (CTGAN) and the tabular variational autoencoder (TVAE)~\cite{xu2019ctgan}, learn the distribution from the same few records they are meant to complement\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. They can only recombine patterns already present }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, recombining patterns that already exist }\DIFaddend in those records, for example by interpolating between two existing patients\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and cannot introduce knowledge that the records do not reflect}\DIFdelend ~\cite{cover2006elements}. Large language models (LLMs) offer a \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{different source of information}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{new perspective to address such issue}\DIFaddend . They are pre-trained on large text corpora, which gives them domain knowledge about healthcare variables, such as plausible ranges and typical relations between them, and they can generate new records from a few real examples placed in the prompt, without any training on the target dataset~\cite{borisov2023great,seedat2024cllm}. Their output, however, is free text rather than a dataset: a response may contain fewer records than requested, malformed records, missing or invented columns, or values of the wrong type (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser})\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and the cost of each request is usually not recorded}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. Every augmentation method is configured with the number of synthetic records to add, usually expressed as a proportion of the real training set~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{chawla2002smote}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, and a generator that silently returns fewer or malformed records changes the training data without the user noticing. In addition, each request to a commercial LLM has a monetary cost, proportional to the number of tokens (word fragments) processed, which is rarely reported}\DIFaddend . One problem that stands out is therefore how to obtain from an LLM a synthetic dataset \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{that has the }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{with the configured }\DIFaddend number of records\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{requested by the user, conforms to }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, }\DIFaddend the columns and types of the real data and \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{has }\DIFdelend a known cost, so that it can be used to improve the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{performance of }\DIFdelend classifiers trained on small data. \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Formally, given the training records $D$ of a small dataset, their columns and types $S$ and the configured number of synthetic records $N$, a configuration $\theta$ of the generator produces a synthetic dataset $G_\theta$. The goal is to find a configuration whose dataset belongs to the set $\mathcal{F}(S,N)$ of datasets with exactly $N$ records that conform to $S$ and that maximizes the gain in classifier performance $\Delta U$, while keeping the distance from the real distribution $L_{\mathrm{fid}}$, the privacy risk $L_{\mathrm{priv}}$ and the cost $\kappa$ low:
}\begin{equation}
\theta^{\star}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta:\,G_{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}(S,N)}
\bigl(-\Delta U,\;L_{\mathrm{fid}},\;L_{\mathrm{priv}},\;\kappa\bigr)(\theta).
\label{eq:multiobjective}
\end{equation}
\DIFadd{The constraint follows the data-quality dimensions of completeness and conformity~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{wang1996beyond,batini2009methodologies}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, and the objectives are the utility, fidelity and privacy on which synthetic data are evaluated~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{qian2023synthcity,lautrup2025syntheval}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, together with cost.
}\DIFaddend 

Currently, deep generators~\cite{xu2019ctgan,kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn} and language models fine-tuned on the target data~\cite{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer} must be trained on the scarce records, and they were designed and evaluated on datasets with tens of thousands of records. Approaches that prompt an LLM directly, such as CLLM~\cite{seedat2024cllm} and EPIC~\cite{kim2024epic}, avoid training, but they focus on the design of the prompt and on the selection of the generated records; they do not ensure that the output \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{is a valid dataset of the requested size}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{has the configured number of records and the correct columns and types}\DIFaddend , and they do not \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{measure }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{report }\DIFaddend its cost. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{In addition}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Finally}\DIFaddend , generators are usually compared on \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{the result of }\DIFaddend a single run, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{although }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{which is unreliable }\DIFaddend in small data\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{the variation between }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. A small test set amplifies chance: with 40 test records, one misclassified record changes accuracy by 2.5 percentage points, and the randomness of the data split and of the LLM's sampling makes }\DIFaddend two runs of the same generator \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{can be as large as the difference between two generators}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{differ by amounts comparable to the differences being compared}\DIFaddend .

To address these limitations, we propose \system \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, a system that separates the generation of records, performed by the LLM, from their admission into the synthetic dataset, performed by }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{(}\emph{\DIFadd{synthetic data agent}}\DIFadd{), an SDG system for small healthcare datasets that combines a pre-trained LLM, which proposes new records, with }\DIFaddend a \emph{control layer}\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{: }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, }\DIFaddend a deterministic software component \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{placed between the LLM and the dataset, which }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{that }\DIFaddend checks every response of the LLM before any of its records is accepted \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{into the synthetic dataset}\DIFaddend . The control layer selects the real records shown to the model, repairs and validates every response and asks for corrections, requests exactly the missing records, and logs the cost of every request. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{The name }%DIFDELCMD < \system %%%
\DIFdel{(}\emph{\DIFdel{synthetic data agent}}%DIFAUXCMD
\DIFdel{) reflects this design: like }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Like }\DIFaddend an LLM agent, the system \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{thus }\DIFaddend alternates actions, the requests to the LLM, with observations, the checks of their output\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, which gives the system its name}\DIFaddend . This point is crucial since it makes the domain knowledge of LLMs usable where established generators are weakest, and since \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{a dataset with the requested size, valid columns and types, and a known cost }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{each healthcare facility can run the system on its own data and obtain a dataset that }\DIFaddend can be audited like any other data source.

The main contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \system, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{a system for LLM-based SDG in small-data contexts }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{an SDG system for small healthcare datasets }\DIFaddend that requires no training on the target data \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{;
  }%DIFDELCMD < \item %%%
\item%DIFAUXCMD
\DIFdel{a control layerthat guarantees the requested }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{and, through its control layer, delivers the configured }\DIFaddend number of records \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and their conformity to }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{with }\DIFaddend the columns and types of the real data \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and records the costof every request}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{and a recorded cost;
  }\item \DIFadd{a deployment pattern in which each healthcare facility runs }\system \DIFadd{locally and only model updates are shared, so that no record leaves the facility}\DIFaddend ;
  \item an open-source implementation that works with any OpenAI-compatible LLM endpoint, including locally served models.
\end{itemize}

\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Four }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Our objective is to determine whether }\system \DIFadd{makes LLM-based SDG a reliable and useful option for small healthcare datasets. To this end, four }\DIFaddend main research questions emerged in this work:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.8em,labelsep=.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item[\resq{1}] Is there any relationship between the control layer and the reliability of the generated datasets?
  \item[\resq{2}] Are the generated records faithful to the real data without reproducing them?
  \item[\resq{3}] Do the generated records improve the performance of classifiers trained on small data?
  \item[\resq{4}] How does \system compare with established generation strategies, and how much do its results depend on the LLM?
\end{itemize}

To answer these questions, an experimental setup was built with six public healthcare datasets containing between 195 and 768 records. For each dataset, synthetic records were produced by \system with four commercial LLMs, by direct prompting of the same LLM and by three established generation strategies, SMOTE, CTGAN and TVAE. Each dataset was randomly divided ten times into a training and a test set, and on each division ten classifiers were trained with and without synthetic records and compared on the same test set. The \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{answers are , in short, }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{main conclusions are }\DIFaddend the following. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{RQ1: yes; direct prompting }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Without the control layer, the LLM }\DIFaddend never returned the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{requested }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{configured }\DIFaddend number of records, whereas \system completed every dataset \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{to that number at a cost }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{at }\DIFaddend only 1.9\% \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{higher. RQ2: the generated }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{more cost (RQ1). The synthetic }\DIFaddend records reproduced the distribution of each real feature with a fidelity of 73 out of 100, and a membership-inference attack could not reliably identify the real records used, although a classifier could still \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{distinguish }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{tell }\DIFaddend synthetic from real records \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. RQ3: the effect }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{(RQ2). The effect on classifiers }\DIFaddend depended on the dataset and the classifier\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{; }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, and }\DIFaddend with the strongest LLM \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, }\DIFdelend the average F1-score increased by 1.09 percentage points \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. RQ4: }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{(RQ3). }\DIFaddend \system gave the largest and most stable \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{F1 }\DIFdelend gain among the generators compared\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. Synthetic }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{: synthetic }\DIFaddend records can also \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{lower the performance of }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{harm }\DIFaddend a classifier when they misrepresent the real data\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{; with }%DIFDELCMD < \system %%%
\DIFdel{the largest such loss on any dataset was 0.53 points, against }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, and the established generators caused losses of }\DIFaddend up to 10.6 points \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{for the established generators. Only }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{on individual datasets, against at most 0.53 points for }\system\DIFadd{; only }\DIFaddend the choice of LLM changed the results by more than the variation between repeated runs \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{(RQ4)}\DIFaddend .

\DIFaddend \subsection{Synthetic Data Generation for Tabular Data}
Tabular SDG methods fall into four families, which differ in how they produce new records. \emph{Interpolation and statistical methods} derive new records directly from the real ones or from a simple probabilistic model of them: SMOTE places a new minority-class record on the line segment between two neighbouring records of that class~\cite{chawla2002smote}, and Bayesian networks estimate the dependencies between features and sample new records from them~\cite{zhang2014privbayes}. \emph{Deep generative models} train a neural network to approximate the joint distribution of the features and then sample from it: CTGAN trains a generator against a discriminator, TVAE learns a compressed representation of the records~\cite{xu2019ctgan}, and diffusion models such as TabDDPM, CoDi and TabSyn learn to reverse a gradual corruption of the records~\cite{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,lee2023codi,zhang2024tabsyn}; relational and cross-silo variants extend these models to several tables or institutions~\cite{fan2020relational,shankar2024silofuse}. \emph{Fine-tuned language models} write each record as a sentence, fine-tune a language model on these sentences and generate new ones, as in GReaT~\cite{borisov2023great}, REaLTabFormer~\cite{solatorio2023realtabformer} and TabLoft~\cite{chen2026tabloft}. \emph{Prompted LLMs} place a few real records in the prompt of a pre-trained LLM and ask it for new ones, without any training, as in CLLM~\cite{seedat2024cllm} and EPIC~\cite{kim2024epic}.

\subsection{Synthetic Data Generation in Small Data}
Most of the previously mentioned generators were designed and benchmarked on datasets with tens or hundreds of thousands of records, such as Adult (48,842 records) or Covertype (581,012 records)~\cite{xu2019ctgan}, and their behaviour on a few hundred records has received far less attention. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes, for each family, how records are produced and its main limitation when data are small. Interpolation and statistical methods, as well as deep generators, are fitted only to the real records, so they cannot add information beyond what those records contain~\cite{cover2006elements}\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and they ignore what the features mean}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. They also treat every column as an anonymous number: they do not know that a column holds a patient's age or cholesterol level, and therefore cannot use what is known about these variables}\DIFaddend . Deep generators and fine-tuned language models \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{must, in addition, estimate many parameters from few records}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{have many parameters by design, which a few hundred records cannot estimate reliably}\DIFaddend , so they \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{can }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{may }\DIFaddend produce records outside the observed ranges and types, or reproduce training records~\cite{hyeong2022tabularmia}. Prompted LLMs need no training and bring domain knowledge, but nothing ensures that their output \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{is a valid dataset of the requested size, and its cost is not measured}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{contains the configured number of records with the correct columns and types, and the cost of obtaining it is not reported}\DIFaddend ; CLLM~\cite{seedat2024cllm} filters the generated records by their learning dynamics and EPIC~\cite{kim2024epic} designs balanced prompts, but both concern the quality of the records that are kept rather than the size, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{validity }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{structure }\DIFaddend and cost of the output. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Across all families , finally, generators are }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, all families are also }\DIFaddend usually compared on a single run\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, so differences caused by the variation between runs, which is large in small data, are reported as effects}\DIFdelend . \system \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{targets the limitation }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{keeps the advantages }\DIFaddend of prompted LLMs \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{with its control layer while keeping their advantages}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{and adds the control layer that they lack}\DIFaddend , and its evaluation \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{measures }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{repeats every experiment to measure }\DIFaddend the variation between \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{repeated runsbefore comparing configurations}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{runs}\DIFaddend .

\subsection{Evaluating Synthetic Data}
Data-preparation studies have shown that a step that looks beneficial can harm downstream models~\cite{li2021cleanml,guha2023cleaningfairness}, and evaluation frameworks such as Synthcity and SynthEval recommend assessing fidelity, privacy and utility together~\cite{qian2023synthcity,lautrup2025syntheval}. Fidelity can be complemented by a classifier two-sample test, which asks whether a classifier can tell real from synthetic records~\cite{lopezpaz2017c2st}, and privacy by membership-inference attacks, which ask whether an attacker can tell which records were used for generation~\cite{shokri2017mia,hyeong2022tabularmia}. Formal guarantees require syntactic anonymization models such as $\ell$-diversity and $t$-closeness~\cite{machanavajjhala2006ldiversity,li2007tcloseness} or differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014dp}, which \system does not claim.

\section{\DIFdel{Problem Formulation}}
%DIFAUXCMD
\addtocounter{section}{-1}%DIFAUXCMD
%DIFDELCMD < \label{sec:problem}
%DIFDELCMD < %%%
%DIF < ======================================================================
\DIFdel{We now state the problem introduced in Section~\ref{sec:intro} precisely. Given a small dataset $D$, its schema $S$ (the features and their types) and a requested number of synthetic records $N$, the goal is to produce, without training a generator on $D$, a synthetic dataset $G$ that satisfies six requirements: it has exactly $N$ records (R1); every record conforms to $S$ (R2); the cost of producing it is measured (R3); it is faithful to the distribution of $D$ (R4); it does not reproduce the records of $D$ (R5); and adding it to the training data improves, or at least does not degrade, the classifiers trained on $D$ (R6). These requirements are not new; they combine two established lines of work. R1 to R3 correspond to data-quality dimensions used in data management, namely completeness, conformity to a schema and the cost of acquiring the data~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{wang1996beyond,batini2009methodologies}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, and they concern the synthetic dataset as a data product: a system can guarantee them. R4 to R6 are the three dimensions on which synthetic data are evaluated in the literature, namely fidelity, privacy and utility~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{qian2023synthcity,lautrup2025syntheval}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, and they concern the value of the synthetic records: they can only be measured. The relation between the two groups is therefore one of order. A dataset that fails R1 or R2 cannot be used at all, so its fidelity, privacy and utility are not even defined, whereas a dataset that satisfies R1 to R3 can then be judged on R4 to R6. Formally, let $D^{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $D^{\mathrm{te}}$ be a training and a test partition of a small dataset $D$ with schema $S$, and let a configuration $\theta$ of the system (LLM, context strategy, active rules) produce a synthetic dataset $G_\theta$ from $D^{\mathrm{tr}}$ without access to $D^{\mathrm{te}}$. Requirements R1 and R2 define the feasible set $\mathcal{F}(S,R,N)$ of datasets with exactly $N$ records that conform to $S$ and to the active rules $R$. The other requirements are objectives: a fidelity loss $L_{\mathrm{fid}}(D^{\mathrm{tr}},G_\theta)$ for R4, a privacy risk $L_{\mathrm{priv}}(D^{\mathrm{tr}},G_\theta)$ for R5, the change in downstream utility $\Delta U(G_\theta)$ for R6 and the generation cost $\kappa(\theta)$, which R3 requires to be measured and which should also be kept low:
}%DIFDELCMD < \begin{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < \theta^{\star}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta:\,G_{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}(S,R,N)}
%DIFDELCMD < \bigl(L_{\mathrm{fid}},\;L_{\mathrm{priv}},\;-\Delta U,\;\kappa\bigr)(\theta).
%DIFDELCMD < \label{eq:multiobjective}
%DIFDELCMD < \end{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < %%%
\DIFdel{The objectives in Equation~\eqref{eq:multiobjective} can conflict: records closer to the real ones are more faithful but easier to attack, and a larger LLM may produce better records at a higher cost. We therefore do not collapse them into one weighted score, because the right weights depend on the use; a healthcare facility sharing data for software testing weights privacy and validity, whereas a team augmenting a classifier weights utility. The formulation also divides the work of the paper. The constraint $G_\theta\in\mathcal{F}(S,R,N)$ is the responsibility of the system, which the next section describes; the objectives are the subject of the evaluation that follows it.
}\DIFdelend

\section{The \system System}
\label{sec:system}
%======================================================================
\system meets the constraint of Equation~\eqref{eq:multiobjective} by checking the output of the LLM instead of relying on it: every response is verified, and corrected or completed when necessary, before its records enter the synthetic dataset\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{; the real dataset is never modified}\DIFaddend . The architecture that implements this principle is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}. A profiling step infers the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{schema $S$ and the type rules $R$ }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{columns and types $S$ }\DIFaddend from the training \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{data}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{records}\DIFaddend . Each request then passes through four stages: the context selector builds the prompt (1), the LLM proposes a batch of records (2), the parser and validator check it (3), and the accepted records are counted against the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{requested size }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{configured number $N$ }\DIFaddend (4). \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Each type of failure triggers its own correction. We describe first the state that ties these stagestogether and then each stage in turn}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{The fixed parameters of these stages, and the reason for each value, are listed in Table~\ref{tab:params}; they were set before the experiments and not tuned}\DIFaddend .

\DIFaddend \subsection{State of the Control Layer}
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Let $N$ be the requested number of synthetic records and $R$ the active rules. }\DIFdelend At step $t$, the control layer keeps the state
\DIFdelbegin %DIFDELCMD < \begin{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < z_t=(S,\,R,\,C_t,\,A_t,\,N-|A_t|,\,B_t,\,E_t),
%DIFDELCMD < \label{eq:state}
%DIFDELCMD < \end{equation}%%%
\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \begin{equation}
z_t=(S,\,C_t,\,A_t,\,N-|A_t|,\,B_t,\,E_t),
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}\DIFaddend 
where $C_t$ is the set of real records shown to the LLM, $A_t$ the accepted synthetic records, $N-|A_t|$ the number still missing, $B_t$ the remaining token budget and $E_t$ the log of parser and validator results. The LLM, denoted $\pi_{\phi}$, proposes a batch $Y_t\sim\pi_{\phi}(\cdot\mid z_t)$. Let $P_S$ be the parser, which also maps the returned \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{features }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{columns }\DIFaddend to $S$, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{$V_{S,R}$ }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{$V_S$ }\DIFaddend the validator and $K$ a correction step that sends invalid values back to the LLM\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, at most twice}\DIFdelend . The accepted set is updated as
\DIFdelbegin %DIFDELCMD < \begin{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < A_{t+1}=A_t\cup K_{V_{S,R}}\bigl(P_S(Y_t)\bigr).
%DIFDELCMD < \label{eq:acceptance}
%DIFDELCMD < \end{equation}%%%
\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \begin{equation}
A_{t+1}=A_t\cup K_{V_S}\bigl(P_S(Y_t)\bigr).
\label{eq:acceptance}
\end{equation}\DIFaddend 
Equation~\eqref{eq:acceptance} expresses the central design decision: the LLM only proposes, and a record enters $A_{t+1}$ only after it has been parsed, mapped to $S$ and validated. Because the state is explicit rather than implied by the conversation history, the number of missing records is always known, and $E_t$ records every failure for later inspection.

\DIFaddend \subsection{Deployment Across Healthcare Facilities}
\label{sec:deployment}
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{The setting that motivates this work, small datasets held by healthcare facilities that cannot pool them}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, healthcare facilities often cannot pool their records}\DIFaddend ~\cite{rieke2020digitalhealth,sheller2020medicine}, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{also suggests how }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{and }\DIFaddend \system \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{can be deployed}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{was designed for this setting}\DIFaddend . Because the control layer needs only a local dataset, its schema and an inference endpoint, each healthcare facility of a network can run it inside its own infrastructure. Consider $K$ healthcare facilities, each holding a small dataset $D_k$ for the same prediction task, with different case mix, coding practice and class prevalence. Each healthcare facility runs \system locally and trains on its real and synthetic records,
\DIFdelbegin %DIFDELCMD < \begin{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < G_k=\mathcal{A}(D_k,S_k,R_k,B_k),\qquad
%DIFDELCMD < \Delta w_k=\mathcal{U}(w_t,D_k\cup G_k),
%DIFDELCMD < \label{eq:local-federated}
%DIFDELCMD < \end{equation}%%%
\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \begin{equation}
G_k=\mathcal{A}(D_k,S_k,N_k),\qquad
\Delta w_k=\mathcal{U}(w_t,D_k\cup G_k),
\label{eq:local-federated}
\end{equation}\DIFaddend 
where $S_k$ \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and $R_k$ }\DIFdelend are the local \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{schema and rules, $B_k$ the local token budget }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{columns and types, $N_k$ the local number of synthetic records }\DIFaddend and $\mathcal{U}$ the local training procedure. Only the model update $\Delta w_k$ and approved aggregate diagnostics leave the healthcare facility. A coordinator aggregates the updates as in FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg,kairouz2021federated},
\begin{equation}
w_{t+1}=w_t+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_t}\frac{n_k}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_t}n_j}\,\Delta w_k,\qquad n_k=|D_k|,
\label{eq:federated-aggregate}
\end{equation}
preferably under secure aggregation~\cite{bonawitz2017secure}. The weights count \emph{real} records, $|D_k|$ rather than $|D_k\cup G_k|$, so a healthcare facility gains no extra weight by generating more records (Figure~\ref{fig:federated}). Unlike SiloFuse, which trains a shared generator across sites~\cite{shankar2024silofuse}, this design needs no generator training across healthcare facilities, and each healthcare facility can reject its synthetic records when paired local validation shows no gain. The design is not, by itself, a privacy guarantee: with a remote LLM the prompt content leaves the healthcare facility, so sensitive deployments need a locally served model or a data-processing agreement, and both synthetic records and model updates can leak information~\cite{chen2024modeltrip}. Our experiments evaluate the generation step one healthcare facility at a time, which is the setting the next section describes; a \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{multi-site }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{multi-facility }\DIFaddend evaluation is future work.

\subsection{Protocol}

\subsection{Measures}
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Each requirement of the problem statement has its own measure. For }\DIFdelend \emph{\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{reliability}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Reliability}\DIFaddend } \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{(R1--R3), we record }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{is measured by }\DIFaddend the number of records delivered against the number \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{requested}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{configured}\DIFaddend , the number of malformed responses, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{completeness (}\DIFdelend the percentage of non-empty values\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{)}\DIFdelend , the corrections made \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{by the control layer }\DIFdelend and the cost in tokens\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and generation time. For }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. }\DIFaddend \emph{\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{fidelity}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Fidelity}\DIFaddend } \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{(R4), we compare }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{compares }\DIFaddend the synthetic records alone \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, without the real context, }\DIFdelend with the real training records over the numerical features $\mathcal{J}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{JSD}}&=100\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}
\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}D_{\mathrm{JS}}(D_j,G_j)\Bigr),\\
F_{\mathrm{corr}}&=100\bigl(1-\operatorname{mean}|\Sigma_D-\Sigma_G|\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\mathrm{JS}}$ is the Jensen--Shannon divergence between real and synthetic \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{marginals }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{distributions of a feature }\DIFaddend and $\Sigma_D$, $\Sigma_G$ are \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{their }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{the }\DIFaddend correlation matrices; both scores equal 100 for identical distributions. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{We add a }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{A }\DIFaddend classifier two-sample test (C2ST)~\cite{lopezpaz2017c2st} \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, in which }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{trains }\DIFaddend a random forest \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{is trained }\DIFdelend to separate real from synthetic records\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{and }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{; }\DIFaddend an accuracy close to 0.5 means \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{that }\DIFaddend they are hard to tell apart\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, and, for }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. For }\DIFaddend the established generators and direct prompting \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{we also report }\DIFaddend the percentage of \emph{valid} records, whose values lie in the observed ranges and categories and are integers where the real feature is, a stricter criterion than \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{R2, which concerns features and types only. For }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{the column-and-type checks of the control layer. }\DIFaddend \emph{\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{privacy}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Privacy}\DIFaddend } \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{(R5), }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{is measured by }\DIFaddend a membership-inference attack \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{that }\DIFaddend scores each real record by its distance to the nearest synthetic record and reports the ROC AUC between training and test records, where 0.5 is chance. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{For }\DIFdelend \emph{\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{utility}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Utility}\DIFaddend } \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{(R6), }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{is }\DIFaddend the paired change\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, }\DIFaddend for metric $m\in\{\mathrm{accuracy},\mathrm{F1}\}$, dataset $d$, classifier $c$ and repetition $h$\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{is
}%DIFDELCMD < \begin{equation}
%DIFDELCMD < \Delta m_{d,c,h}=m\bigl(D^{\mathrm{tr}}_{d,h}\cup G_{d,h};\,c\bigr)-m\bigl(D^{\mathrm{tr}}_{d,h};\,c\bigr),
%DIFDELCMD < \label{eq:utility}
%DIFDELCMD < \end{equation}%%%
\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{,
}\begin{equation}
\Delta m_{d,c,h}=m\bigl(D_{d,h}\cup G_{d,h};\,c\bigr)-m\bigl(D_{d,h};\,c\bigr),
\label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}\DIFaddend 
with both models scored on the same test \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{partition, so that variation between test partitions is not mistaken for an effect of SDG}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{set}\DIFaddend . We use binary F1 for the binary tasks and class-weighted F1 for heart disease\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{; binary F1 scores the class coded 1, which is the minority class for heart failure and diabetes and the majority class for liver disease and Parkinson's. Changes are reported }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, and report changes }\DIFaddend in percentage points (pp).

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
Repetitions and classifiers are not independent datasets, so \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{we average them }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{they are averaged }\DIFaddend within each dataset and \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{treat }\DIFdelend the six datasets \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{are treated }\DIFaddend as matched blocks. Configurations are compared with the Friedman test,
\begin{equation}
\chi^2_F=\frac{12M}{Q(Q+1)}\sum_{a=1}^{Q}\bar{\mathrm{rk}}_a^{\,2}-3M(Q+1),
\label{eq:friedman}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathrm{rk}}_a$ is the average rank of configuration $a$ over $M=6$ datasets and $Q$ the number of configurations, followed by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of the best-ranked configuration against every other, with Holm correction~\cite{demsar2006statistical,holm1979procedure}. \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{Per-cell tests of augmented versus real-only training over the ten repetitions are Holm-corrected within each metric. }\DIFdelend With six blocks the tests have limited power, \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{which is precisely why we report }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{so }\DIFaddend effect sizes and the noise floor \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{are reported }\DIFaddend alongside $p$-values.
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{With these measures in place, we can now answer the research questions in order.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================
\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{We set out }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{The objective of this work was }\DIFaddend to determine whether \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{a control layer }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \system \DIFaddend makes LLM-based SDG a reliable and useful option for small \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{data, and the four answers combine into a clear picture}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{healthcare datasets}\DIFaddend . Reliability is achieved, and it is achieved by the control layer: the same LLM, prompted directly, never \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{returned the dataset that was requested}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{delivered the configured dataset}\DIFaddend , whereas \system \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{completes itby construction, records }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{completed it, recorded }\DIFaddend its cost and caught the failures of a less disciplined model \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, }\DIFdelend at almost no extra cost (RQ1). Usefulness is real but bounded: the synthetic records are faithful without \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{reproducing real ones , yet distinguishable from them }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{revealing real ones }\DIFaddend (RQ2)\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{; they help }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, they help the }\DIFaddend classifiers that fail on the minority class (RQ3)\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{; }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, }\DIFaddend and with a stronger LLM they give the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{most consistent F1 }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{largest and most stable }\DIFaddend gain among the generators compared (RQ4). No generator, however, improves \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{utility significantly on all datasets. This }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{every dataset, which }\DIFaddend is consistent with the data-processing inequality~\cite{cover2006elements}: SDG cannot replace missing patients, and its benefit is bounded by the knowledge the generator brings. Among the generators \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{we compared, the LLM is the only one that }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{compared, only the LLM }\DIFaddend brings such knowledge, and the control layer is what makes it usable. \DIFdelbegin %DIFDELCMD < 

%DIFDELCMD < %%%
\DIFdel{These results translate into four practical recommendations. Choose the LLM first, since it is the only factor whose effect exceeds the variation between runs. Measure cost in tokens rather than time, which depends on the provider. Check }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{In practice, the choice of LLM matters most, cost is better measured in tokens than in time, and }\DIFaddend the effect of synthetic records \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{per classifier, on paired splits, before using them. The conclusions are subject to several limitations. Only six datasets act as independent blocks, which limits the power of the tests, and the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{splits }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{divisions }\DIFaddend are not stratified. The columns and types are inferred }\DIFaddend from the full dataset \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, which exposes test extremes to the profiler; train-only profiling would be stricter. JSD uses fixed histograms and is dominated by numerical features, and the membership attack is a single }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{rather than from the training set alone. The parameters in Table~\ref{tab:params} were fixed and not tuned, and only two of them were varied, through the LLM-chosen settings variant. }\system \DIFadd{does not filter synthetic records that are close to real ones: privacy is measured with one }\DIFaddend distance-based \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{adversary, so }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{attack, not enforced, and }\DIFaddend no result establishes differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014dp}. \DIFdel{. The correction paths }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{. \section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================
Small \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{clinical }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{healthcare }\DIFaddend datasets pose a dilemma for synthetic data generation\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{. Generators }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{: generators }\DIFaddend fitted to the available records \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{cannot add the information those records lack}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{can only recombine the patterns those records contain}\DIFaddend , whereas an LLM can \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{bring knowledge from pre-training }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{add domain knowledge }\DIFaddend but returns text rather than a dataset. \system resolves the dilemma with a control layer that turns \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{LLM responses into complete, validated datasets of the requested size and records their }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{the responses of an LLM into complete synthetic datasets with the columns and types of the real data and a recorded }\DIFaddend cost, without training on the target data. On six \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{small }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{public }\DIFaddend healthcare datasets, direct prompting never delivered the \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{requested }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{configured }\DIFaddend dataset, while \system \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{completes it by construction, }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{did }\DIFaddend at 1.9\% more \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{tokens; with the larger, more recent }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{cost, and with the strongest }\DIFaddend LLM it gave the largest and most \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{consistent }\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{stable }\DIFaddend F1 gain of five generators\DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{, although no generator was significantly better on every dataset}\DIFdelend . Because repeated runs of the same pipeline vary as much as most design choices, we recommend that SDG for small data be evaluated \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{against such a noise floor}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{on repeated runs}\DIFaddend . Future work will evaluate locally served LLMs, class-aware context selection\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{, privacy filters }\DIFaddend and the deployment across healthcare facilities outlined in Section~\ref{sec:deployment}.
