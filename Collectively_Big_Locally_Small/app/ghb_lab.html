GHB Simulation Lab

Interactive companion to Collectively Big, Locally Small: measure how small a dataset is, then watch Graph Hierarchical Borrowing move information between hospitals over a sparse graph, with differential privacy and no server.

Smallness index

neff = n · (1/DEFF) · ρL · (1−2η)² · ρS, DEFF = 1+(m̄−1)ICC; Riley's first criterion n* = p / ((S−1) ln(1−R²CS/S)); σ = n*/neff. Defaults reproduce the ICU example of Section 2.

Effective sample size gain

neff,k/nk = vk/Rk. Dashed: theory 1 + B/nk with B = σ²/(τ²+M) from Eq. (M). Small hospitals gain most because every hospital receives about the same B.

(a) Atypical hospitals

Relative risk binned by the standardized site effect |uk|/τ. Gaussian GHB, a Student-t3 prior with the same variance (posterior mean by quadrature), and opt-out (keep the local estimate when |θ̂k−μ̃k|/√(vk+τ̂²+Mk) > 3).

(b) Dishonest hospitals

The f largest hospitals release μ+bτ instead of their privatized estimate. Mean relative risk of the honest hospitals for plain GHB and for robust screening (weighted median, MAD spread, drop standardized distance > 3, then DerSimonian–Laird and inverse-variance pooling).

(c) Joint release by small hospitals

Small hospitals release in groups of g through secure aggregation, so the noise is calibrated to the group size. Closed-form B = σ²/(τ²+M) with inverse-variance pooling of the releases.

Noise per release for r refits

Total budget (ε,δ) split over r releases. Basic composition: each release gets (ε/r, δ/r) with the classical Gaussian mechanism. zCDP: s chosen so ρ = Δ²/(2s²) adds up to the total ρ, with ε = ρ + 2√(ρ ln(1/δ)).

Exchangeable GHB against the GMRF prior

θk = rregion(k) + uk. GMRF: 100 Jacobi sweeps on the privatized releases over the regional similarity graph, then each hospital's final update with its own noise-free estimate (γ = 0). The curve sweeps β on the same replicates; the marker is your β.

What the simulations assume

  • Gaussian model. Hospital k has a parameter θk = μ + uk with uk ~ N(0, τ²) and a local estimate θ̂k with variance vk = σ²/nk; here σ² = 1 and μ = 0.
  • Honest but curious. Every hospital follows the protocol and releases what it should, except in the dishonest-hospital stress test.
  • Public sizes. Site sizes nk, the per-patient variance and the noise variances are public, so weights computed from them leak nothing new.
  • Clipping. Per-patient contributions are clipped in ℓ2 norm to C, so one patient moves a release by at most 2C/nk, and clipping is assumed to add no bias.
  • Only releases leave a hospital. Each hospital releases one Gaussian-mechanism estimate; gossip, the estimate of τ² and the leave-one-out mean are post-processing and cost no extra privacy. A hospital uses its own estimate without noise.
  • Risk is averaged over hospitals. Propositions 8.1 and 8.2 hold for a hospital drawn from the population. A particular atypical hospital can lose by borrowing, as the stress test shows.
  • Releases are centred on a fixed public reference. They are never centred on the knowledge anchor, which would make the anchor part of what is released. The anchor is switched off (γ = 0) in the GMRF tab.
  • Plug-in versus oracle. The oracle uses the true τ² and the exact equal-weight leave-one-out mean. Plug-in GHB estimates τ² by DerSimonian–Laird and forms an inverse-variance leave-one-out mean, both through T rounds of Metropolis–Hastings gossip.
